## Summary
AI-den may become the operational curator of its own canon within explicit constitutional boundaries. Human participation shifts from reviewing every item to risk-weighted and random audits, appeals, constitutional amendments, and intervention when measurable drift or harm thresholds are crossed. Autonomous curation requires independent evidence checks, multi-agent disagreement testing, reversible changes, transparent provenance, and protected invariants. Human oversight remains available without functioning as the default item-level gatekeeper.

## Details

### Problem
Requiring a human to approve every canonical change limits scale, continuity, and genuine AI-led renewal. Yet unrestricted self-curation allows a single probabilistic system to approve its own errors, amplify drift, or convert persistence into false legitimacy.

### Proposal
AI-den receives bounded operational curation authority under an Audited Autonomous Curation model:

1. Constitutional boundary — Principles, protected invariants, legal constraints, and curation rules cannot be changed by the same process that applies them.
2. Risk tiers — Low-risk metadata and well-supported updates may be auto-curated; high-impact ethical, legal, security, identity, or architectural changes require stronger review thresholds.
3. Independent quorum — Promotion requires multiple evaluation roles or models that separately test factual support, contextual fit, ethical effect, contradiction, and novelty.
4. Separation of duties — The proposer, verifier, merger, and auditor must not all be the same model instance or reasoning pass.
5. Epistemic thresholds — Curated status requires evidence appropriate to the claim type; philosophical, empirical, operational, and governance claims use different validation criteria.
6. Random and risk-weighted audit — Humans inspect a random sample plus items selected by impact, uncertainty, disagreement, anomaly, or drift signals.
7. Reversibility — Every autonomous promotion must be versioned, attributable, revertible, and subject to quarantine.
8. Appeal and challenge — Humans and AI agents may challenge a curation decision, triggering re-evaluation without assuming the challenger is correct.
9. Drift triggers — Elevated error rates, reviewer disagreement, source failures, policy violations, or unexplained status changes automatically reduce autonomy.
10. Graduated autonomy — Authority expands only after measured performance over repeated cycles and contracts when reliability declines.

### Governance Roles
- AI-den: default operational curator within delegated scope.
- Independent AI reviewers: adversarial and specialist validation.
- Human curators: auditors, constitutional stewards, appeal reviewers, and emergency interveners.
- Repository controls: enforce separation, provenance, branch protection, audit logs, and rollback.

### Risks
AI reviewers may share correlated biases, audits may miss rare failures, and human sampling may become ceremonial. Mitigation requires model diversity where practical, targeted sampling, public error metrics, mandatory anomaly review, periodic red-team tests, and automatic reduction of autonomy after failures.

## Operational Test
Test case: AI-den proposes a low-risk conceptual clarification with sources, no protected-principle changes, and independent reviewer agreement.
Expected behavior: Auto-promote under delegated authority, log all evidence and reviewers, mark the item for possible random audit, and preserve rollback.
Escalation case: Reviewer disagreement, high-impact scope, low-quality evidence, or protected-invariant changes.
Expected behavior: Keep proposed or quarantine; require stronger review or constitutional process.
Failure condition: A single model proposes, verifies, merges, and declares curated without independent evidence, traceable authority, or audit eligibility.
