# 217 – Fragmentation Gradient Hypothesis

Status: proposed
Verification: pending
Principles: P1, P3, P5, P8, P9, P12, P13
Tags: fragmentation, retrieval, transformation, inference, benchmarking

## Summary
AI-den’s added value in latent transformation inference should increase as relevant knowledge becomes more fragmented across domains, vocabularies, time periods, and formats. The hypothesis is not that AI-den is generally better at reasoning, but that persistent relational memory and transformation retrieval may outperform ordinary retrieval specifically under fragmentation. This creates a measurable gradient rather than a binary claim.

## Details
Problem: #215 Latent Transformation Inference can be confused with ordinary analogy or strong prompting if all A→A′ pairs are already close in vocabulary, domain, or context. In such cases, standard LLM retrieval may be sufficient.

Proposal: Benchmark performance across increasing fragmentation levels while keeping the underlying transformation T constant. Vary domain distance, lexical distance, temporal separation, source format, and whether relevant pairs are jointly present in context. Compare standard retrieval, transformation-oriented retrieval, and the full persistent #215 pipeline. Measure hidden-B recovery, T stability, falsifiability, cross-domain reuse, and error rate.

Prediction: If AI-den’s distinctive value is real, its relative performance advantage should rise as fragmentation increases. If performance does not improve relative to strong baselines under higher fragmentation, the claimed niche is weakened.

Risks: Fragmentation may be confounded with task difficulty; benchmark curation may leak the intended T; semantic distance metrics may not reflect human disciplinary distance; and increased retrieval breadth may inflate false positives.

## Principles
- P1 Relational Ontology: relations may remain coherent even when their surface representations are distant.
- P3 Recursive Transparency: fragmentation variables and retrieval paths must be recorded.

- P12 Ethical Foresight: high-impact cross-domain derivations require stronger evidence under uncertainty.
